\section{Analysis}
\subsection{Top 3 Key Factors}
Based on SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis of the XGBRegressor model, the following three factors have the highest mean absolute contribution to transaction price:

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Area (SQFT)} (SHAP: 0.1683) - Property size is the primary price driver, with larger units commanding proportionally higher values, reflecting Singapore's space premium.

\item \textbf{Project Name} (SHAP: 0.1076) - Captures developer brand premium and development quality. Established projects command higher prices due to reputation and amenity standards.

\item \textbf{Type of Sale} (SHAP: 0.1013) - Distinguishes primary market (new launches) from secondary market (resale), reflecting different pricing dynamics. New launches typically command premiums due to modern amenities and market positioning.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Patterns \& Trends}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Location premium}: Prime districts (6, 7, 9, 10 - CCR areas) command 2.5-3x prices compared to OCR districts (17, 18, 27, 28), demonstrating Singapore's location-based pricing tiers [Section 2.6].

\item \textbf{Seasonal effects}: Q4 (especially November) shows 8-12\% price premiums with peak transaction volumes, suggesting strategic timing for sellers [Section 3.2].

\item \textbf{Tenure impact}: Long-term leasehold dominates (59.6\%), followed by freehold (29.1\%). Freehold properties command an 18.5\% median price premium (\$1.80M vs \$1.52M) despite strong functional equivalence for buyers with 20-30 year horizons [Section 3.1].

\item \textbf{Market segmentation}: 71.2\% of properties fall under \$2M, with only 2.3\% exceeding \$5M, indicating concentrated mid-market activity [Fig 4].

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Buyer Recommendations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Target emerging districts}: Focus on districts 17, 18, 27, 28 offering average \$1.2M properties, representing 50\%+ savings versus premium districts 6, 7, 9, 10 [Section 2.6].
    
    \item \textbf{Time purchases strategically}: Avoid November (peak prices). Purchase during August, December, or January when median prices drop 5-8\% due to lower demand during festive seasons and school year transitions [Section 3.2].
    
    \item \textbf{Optimize size}: Target the 1,000-1,500 sqft range for optimal value at \$1,535 per sqft. Avoid units under 500 sqft (scarcity premium at \$1,746 per sqft) and properties over 5,000 sqft (luxury premium at \$2,281+ per sqft) unless specific needs justify the cost. [Section 2.5].

\end{itemize}
